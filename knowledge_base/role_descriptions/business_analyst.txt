ROLE: Business Analyst
=======================

ROLE OVERVIEW
-------------
A Business Analyst (BA) serves as the bridge between business stakeholders and technical teams. They identify business needs, analyse processes, and translate requirements into specifications that development teams can act upon. A BA ensures that solutions delivered by technology actually solve the underlying business problem. They work across the full project lifecycle, from discovery and requirements gathering to user acceptance testing and post-launch review.

KEY RESPONSIBILITIES
--------------------
- Elicit, document, and validate business and functional requirements through interviews, workshops, and surveys
- Analyse current business processes ("as-is") and design improved future-state processes ("to-be")
- Create business process models using tools such as BPMN, UML, and flowcharts
- Develop requirements artefacts: Business Requirements Document (BRD), Functional Requirements Document (FRD), use cases, user stories, and acceptance criteria
- Build and maintain a requirements traceability matrix (RTM) to link requirements to test cases and deliverables
- Facilitate JAD (Joint Application Development) sessions and requirements workshops
- Perform gap analysis between current and desired system capabilities
- Support user acceptance testing (UAT) and validate that delivered solutions meet requirements
- Identify risks, dependencies, and constraints that affect delivery
- Communicate status, issues, and recommendations to project sponsors and stakeholders

IMPORTANT SKILLS
----------------
Analytical & Technical Skills:
- Requirements elicitation techniques: interviews, focus groups, observation, document analysis
- Process modelling: BPMN 2.0, UML (use case diagrams, activity diagrams, sequence diagrams)
- Documentation: BRD, FRD, user stories, use cases, wireframes, data dictionaries
- Data analysis: SQL basics, Excel/Google Sheets, Power BI or Tableau for reporting
- Agile and Scrum: backlog grooming, sprint planning, writing epics and user stories
- Tools: JIRA, Confluence, MS Visio, Balsamiq, Lucidchart

Business & Stakeholder Skills:
- Stakeholder management and mapping (influence-interest matrix)
- Facilitation of workshops and meetings
- Change management and adoption strategies

Soft Skills:
- Active listening and questioning — the ability to understand unstated needs
- Clear written and verbal communication for both technical and non-technical audiences
- Analytical rigour — decomposing complex problems into structured components
- Negotiation — balancing what stakeholders want with what is feasible

COMMON INTERVIEW FOCUS AREAS
------------------------------
1. Requirements Elicitation: How do you gather requirements when stakeholders are not sure what they want? Know the difference between interviews, workshops, prototyping, and document analysis.
2. SDLC & Methodologies: Explain Waterfall vs. Agile vs. Hybrid. When does each make sense? What is your role in each?
3. Use Cases vs. User Stories: When do you use each and how do you write acceptance criteria (Given-When-Then)?
4. Business Process Modelling: Describe how you document an as-is process and identify improvement opportunities using BPMN.
5. Data Modelling & SQL: Be prepared for basic SQL questions and entity-relationship diagram discussions.
6. Gap Analysis: Describe a gap analysis you performed. How did you prioritise the gaps?
7. Stakeholder Conflict: How do you handle conflicting requirements from two senior stakeholders with equal authority?
8. UAT & Sign-off: How do you structure user acceptance testing and obtain formal sign-off?
